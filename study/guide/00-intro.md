# How to use this guide {#intro}

This guide is built for the **Red Seal Heavy Duty Equipment Technician exam**. It takes Matéo Lusson's HET notes from [first period](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1d4i-LUwBxKAp6eGknbBiKeH2Uynhc1iVBlNId50XqtE), [second period](https://docs.google.com/document/d/15VblJPRHPYERDUBcy_D2WHyvFpmyMo2YuFi9DrqZ0ng) and [third period](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1Kx0yknuqAoN13r4I1tWwqruwL0g3m-6SA_QDxVSvOqs) (summaries of the Alberta ILMs, version 24/25) and sorts them into the 9 sections and 41 tasks that the Red Seal exam is built from.

Unofficial study help made by an apprentice. It is not from the Red Seal program, AIT, NAIT or SAIT, and it can have mistakes. Your ILMs and the OEM service information are the final word.

Your quiz progress and exam history are saved in your own browser only. Nothing is sent anywhere.

- ==Highlighted== text is what the original notes marked as "has been on tests / likely to be on tests". Learn these first.
- **Bold** is a key term or the part of a fact that gets tested.
- "Exam trap" boxes point out answers that are easy to mix up.
- "Note" boxes correct a typo or contradiction in the source notes.
- A few tasks aren't in the notes yet (the 4th period notes, which cover A/C, on-highway powertrain and truck electrical, are still being written). Those sections are written from standard trade knowledge and are marked with a box. Double check them against your own books.

## The Red Seal exam {#exam}

- ==**135 questions**==, all multiple choice with four options (A to D), exactly one correct answer. They are not trick questions, but the wrong answers are not always obvious.
- ==**70%** is the pass mark.== You get **4 hours**.
- The acronym list at the bottom of this section is handed out with the exam, so you don't have to memorize what DPF or SAHR stands for. You do have to know what they do.
- Questions are written from the **Red Seal Occupational Standard** (it replaced the old National Occupational Analysis, NOA) by people in the trade from across Canada.

### Question types
| Type | Share of the exam | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Knowledge and recall | ==5–15%== | What is meant by the viscosity of an oil? |
| Procedural and application | ==45–55%== | When a hydraulic system malfunctions, what is checked first? (fluid level) |
| Critical thinking | ==35–45%== | What is the result of overfilling a powershift transmission? (overheating) |

> **Exam trap:** only about 1 question in 10 is straight recall. Most of the exam asks what you would **do** (in what order) or what a symptom **means**. When you study a fact, also ask "what would I check first?" and "what happens if this fails?". The quiz tab can filter by question type.

- ==**D Electrical** and **G Hydraulics** are 50 of the 135 questions.== Then **B Engines** (20). Study in that order.
- Each task covers "services, diagnoses and repairs". Expect questions on all three: maintenance intervals and procedures, reading symptoms and test results, and the right repair.

### Strategy
- For cause-and-effect questions, ask: is this a **flow** problem (speed, cycle time) or a **pressure** problem (force, torque)? That one split answers a lot of hydraulic and transmission questions. For electrical: is it an **open**, a **short**, a **short to ground** or **high resistance**?
- Diagnostic order the notes repeat in nearly every system: ==**gather information → talk to the operator → visual inspection (fluid level and condition first) → operational tests at operating temperature → pressure / flow / voltage-drop tests → find the root cause → repair → verify**==.
- "What is checked first?" is almost always the **easiest, cheapest, most likely** thing: fluid level, belt, battery state of charge, fuses, connections, filters.
- Read every option before answering. If two look right, pick the one that fixes the cause, not the symptom.

## Formula sheet {#formulas}

| Formula | Use | Task |
|---|---|---|
| Pressure = Force ÷ Area | Cylinder force, gauge reading, brake chamber force | G-34, G-36 |
| Force = Pressure × Area | Brake chamber output (F = P × A) | C-15 |
| Torque = Force × Lever length | Slack adjuster, torque wrench, spring scale preload | C-15, A-2 |
| Work = Force × Distance; Power = Work ÷ Time | Basic physics (1 hp = 550 ft-lb/s = 746 W) | B-5 |
| HP = (Torque × RPM) ÷ 5252 | Engine dyno horsepower | B-5 |
| Displacement = Bore area × Stroke × Cylinders | Engine size; stroke = 2 × crank throw | B-5 |
| CR = (Swept volume + Clearance volume) ÷ Clearance volume | Compression ratio | B-5 |
| Speed droop % = (High idle − Rated speed) ÷ Rated speed × 100 | Governors | B-10 |
| Hydraulic HP = (GPM × PSI) ÷ 1714 | Hydraulic power | G-34 |
| GPM = (Displacement in³/rev × RPM) ÷ 231 | Pump flow (LPM = cm³/rev × RPM ÷ 1000) | G-34 |
| Volumetric eff. % = Actual flow ÷ Theoretical flow × 100 | Pump wear check (below ~80% = repair or replace) | G-34 |
| Rod side force = (Piston area − Rod area) × Pressure | Cylinder retract force | G-34 |
| Volume = Area × Stroke; Flow = Volume ÷ Time | Cycle time questions | G-34 |
| Beta ratio = Particles in ÷ Particles out | Filter rating (higher = better) | G-34 |
| E = I × R; P = I × E | Ohm's law, watts (fuse sizing) | D-21 |
| Series: Rt = R1 + R2 + …; Parallel: 1/Rt = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + … | Circuit resistance | D-21 |
| Batteries in series: volts add, CCA same; parallel: volts same, CCA add | Battery banks | D-20 |
| Gear ratio = Driven teeth ÷ Drive teeth (written ?:1) | All gearing ("multiply torque, divide RPM") | E-25 |
| Total ratio = Ratio 1 × Ratio 2 × … | Multiple reductions, final drives | E-29 |
| Planetary: carrier "teeth" = Sun + Ring | Planetary ratio | E-26 |
| Open differential: RPM left + RPM right = 2 × carrier RPM | Turning, one wheel spinning | E-28 |
| Air brake leak-down (2 min): supply 2 psi × n; service 2 psi + 2 psi × n | n = number of axles/axle groups behind the steer axle | C-15 |
| Stopping power: × 2 for double weight, × 4 for double speed | Brake energy (both doubled = × 8) | C-15 |
